01 / INTRODUCTION

Why flights?

Aviation is one of the fastest-growing sources of greenhouse-gas emissions and a major contributor to climate change. The tracker records greenhouse-gas emissions associated with air travel undertaken for Wild Impact operations. This includes domestic and international flights, and both direct and connecting routes for fieldwork, meetings, conferences, training and other organisational activities. A model is used to recommend less carbon-intensive aircrafts and operators to reduce the operational footprint.

02 / PURPOSE

Why calculate them?

Air travel contributes to the organisation's operational carbon footprint through the combustion of aviation fuel and the release of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. Calculating flight emissions allows us to monitor and manage our carbon footprint, identify emission-intensive routes and evaluate opportunities to reduce aviation-related emissions. These records also support comparisons between travel periods, destinations and flight operators, informing decisions about future travel arrangements.

03 / METHODOLOGY

How do we calculate emissions?

Flight emissions are calculated using the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) carbon emissions methodology, which estimates CO₂ emissions based on flight distance, aircraft type, and passenger load. Emission factors are applied to account for different aircraft models, cabin classes, and seating layouts per passenger.

04 / INTERPRETATION

How should results be read?

Results are presented in tonnes of CO₂ emissions per flight leg, with breakdowns by route, aircraft type, and passenger allocation. These represent the estimated climate impact attributed to the recorded passenger's journey. Higher values generally indicate longer distances, less crowded airflights, more fuel-intensive aircrafts, or higher cabin classes. Results should be interpreted as estimates rather than precise measurements of emissions contributed by an individual passenger.
