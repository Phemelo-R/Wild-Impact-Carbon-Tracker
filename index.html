Welcome to Wild Impact’s Carbon Tracker — a workspace for calculating greenhouse gas emissions from Wild Impact’s Vehicle fleet, Electric motorcycles, Flight trips, and Boat usage throughout all landscapes across Africa. The tracker is designed to support the organisation’s operational carbon accounting and reporting to donors, partners and internal stakeholders. It is not a public-facing emissions reporting tool, nor is it intended to replace independently verified carbon accounting methodologies. The tracker is intended to be used by Wild Impact staff.

Understanding the Carbon Project

This project started as a way to better understand and manage the carbon footprint of our operations across Africa. The main goal was to reduce the carbon footprint of our operations, and to do so in a way that is transparent, evidence-based and aligned with best practice. We wanted to contribute to the global effort to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and to support the transition to a low-carbon economy, as well as to improve the sustainability of our operations and to demonstrate our commitment to environmental stewardship.

This kicked off with a pilot project to measure the carbon footprint we have saved through the use of electric motorcycles in our operations, and to compare this with the carbon footprint of using petrol motorcycles. The pilot project was successful, highlighting a reduction in greenhouse gas emission through the use of electric motorcycles, We have now expanded the project to include all of our operations across Africa, including flights, boats and vehicles.

Formulas and assumptions used in the calculations are based on the UK DESNZ 2026 fuel factors, ICAO-based flight model, and other relevant sources. The tracker is designed to be a management instrument that makes it easy to track and report on the carbon carbon footprint of our operations, especially when tasked to tracking complex emissions such as those from flights across multiple legs. 01 / CONCEPT

What are greenhouse-gas emissions?

Greenhouse-gas emission refer to the release of gases that trap heat in the atmosphere, contributing to global warming and climate change. These emissions are primarily generated by human activities, such as burning fossil fuels for energy, deforestation, and industrial processes. The main greenhouse gases are Carbon Dioxide (CO₂), Methane (CH₄), and Nitrous Oxide (N₂O). Each of these gases has a different global warming potential, which is a measure of how much heat they can trap in the atmosphere over a specific time period.

02 / PURPOSE

Why calculate them?

We calculate greenhouse-gas emission to understand the environmental impact of our operations and to identify opportunities for reducing our carbon footprint. By quantifying emissions, we can set targets for reduction, track progress over time, and make informed decisions about our activities.

03 / BOUNDARIES

What is included?

Electric motorcycles – Tracks charging emissions of electric motorcycles and the displaced petrol-motorcycle baseline for comparison. The baseline is based on the UK DESNZ 2026 fuel factors and the average fuel consumption of petrol motorcycles.

Flights – Uses the ICAO-based model to calculate emissions from flights, including the impact of diffrent aircraft types, flight distances, and passenger loads.

Boats – Uses measured or recorded petrol volumes and UK DESNZ 2026 fuel factors.

Vehicles – Currently under development.

04 / INTERPRETATION

How should results be read?

These are estimated emissions based on the activity data and calculation methods used. They are not independently verified, however, they provide close to accurate estimates of the greenhouse-gas emissions associated with the recorded activities. There are many factors that can influence the accuracy of the results, including the quality and completeness of the activity data, the assumptions made in the calculations, and the limitations of the models used. It is important to interpret the results with caution and to consider them as part of a broader context of environmental impact assessment. DO NOT fabricate or manipulate activity data to achieve a desired outcome.
